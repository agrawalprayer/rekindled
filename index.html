Complexity from simple rules.

I study how local interaction rules generate complex structures, using programmable self-assembly, discrete mathematics and algorithmic information theory.

I'm a DPhil student at the Rudolf Peierls Centre for Theoretical Physics, University of Oxford. My current project examines the algorithmic principles of evolution through a three-dimensional polycube model of protein-complex self-assembly — how symmetry, designability and assembly complexity shape the structures such rules can reach.

Alongside this I work on graph theory and combinatorics, counting and characterising connectivity-preserving growth processes, and on algorithmic complexity — how information and minimal descriptions constrain which structures can be assembled efficiently.

Latest preprint: Successive vertex orderings of connected graphs, with Abdurrahman Hadi Erturk and Ard Louis (2026).
